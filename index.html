Architecture Student · Creative Developer · Designer · Artist
Exploring the transition toward Computer Science & Engineering

Shadid Ahamed was never satisfied with being only one thing. He was an architecture student, but architecture was only one part of him. He drew, painted, built models, designed websites, experimented with code, imagined games, explored technology, read books, and constantly tried to turn the ideas in his head into something real.

He carried a strange combination of an artist’s sensitivity and an engineer’s curiosity. He could care about the tiniest detail of a drawing while also wondering how to build an entire digital world from scratch. His path was not easy — there were disappointments, pressure, family complications, relationships that cut deep, and long periods of mental and emotional strain. Yet one thing kept returning: he kept trying to build something.

He wanted his work to mean something — not simply to look impressive, but to tell people who he was. He was proud of where he came from, his science background, his drawing achievements, his architectural models, and at the same time he was quietly exploring the boundary between architecture, design, art, and computer science.

If one sentence could hold him, it would be this: Shadid was a person who kept trying to turn imagination into reality — through lines, buildings, colours, code, stories, and ideas. He was not someone who had everything figured out. He was someone who was still becoming.
